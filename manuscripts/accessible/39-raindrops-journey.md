# Raindrop’s Journey

![Cover art: Raindrop’s Journey](../art/accessible/39-raindrops-journey.webp)

Pip was a raindrop who loved the mountain. Each morning, sunlight warmed the hilltop. Pip carried a silver glint from every place he had visited, and his round body grew smaller until its water changed into vapor and rose in a column invisible to the children below.

“That is evaporation,” said Miri. “The Sun gives energy for liquid water to become water vapor.”

Pip rose past hawks, pine trees, and the village roof. The air grew cooler. His neighbors gathered around a speck of dust into cloud droplets. Farther up, some water froze into ice crystals. Together they formed a cloud.

Miri looked up. “Clouds are not just invisible vapor. They can hold visible droplets and ice.”

The cloud drifted east. Its droplets grew as cool air made more water condense. They bumped and merged. When they became heavy enough, gravity pulled them down.

Pip heard the first drop strike a schoolyard. Then another. The cloud opened like a gray umbrella.

* * *

Pip landed between two playground tiles and ran toward a drain. A leaf blocked the gutter. Sora lifted it, and Pip slid into a low corner beneath a basketball hoop. Sand had blocked the way. Miri moved a small stone, and the water slipped through, over the path, and toward a storm grate.

The children followed with a toy boat. Water moved along the slope because gravity pulled it downhill. At the stream, they filled a clear jar with cloudy water. Soil sank. The water above grew clearer, though they had not purified it; sediment had simply settled below it.

Miri pointed to the grate. “Rainwater can move underground through spaces in soil. Some seeps into the hillside. Some returns to a stream or spring.”

She built a model with a sponge, a layer of sand, and a tube. Miri poured water onto the sponge. It seeped down, and a slow drip appeared at the tube.

“Water does not always need a river,” she said. “It can move down through the ground.”

* * *

On the mountain, snow gathered in shaded hollows. During a warm afternoon, sunlight melted some of the ice. Water slid downhill in a stream, and some water changed directly from ice into vapor. Miri made a tray model with a tiny snow mountain. A drop at the top melted and traveled down a channel into a cup.

“Snowmelt is another journey,” she said. “Gravity can pull water downhill.”

At the stream, the children met a frog. It drank from the shallows, and water moved through its body before leaving in urine or evaporating from its skin. A dragonfly drank from a leaf puddle. A willow drew water through its roots and lost some as vapor from its leaves. A bird carried droplets on wet feathers.

“Water also travels through organisms,” Miri said. “It is not only falling, sliding, or sinking.”

A dead branch floated downstream. Miri followed it to a pool where fungi, microbes, and small animals began to use the dead material. Water shared many paths.

* * *

A dry spell shrank the stream. The children carried buckets from the deepest pool, but the level kept falling.

Miri brought a chart. She placed a cold, damp cloth over warm air. Droplets appeared on its surface. She warmed the cloth, and some water moved into the air. Cooling helped water condense; warming helped liquid water evaporate.

Pip felt the stream flow beneath a blockage of dry leaves. The water rose behind it, and small fish had nowhere to go. The children worked with adults to pull a log free, making a side channel. The water rushed through, giving the fish room and carrying leaf litter onward.

Then the rain returned. It fell on the mountain, school roof, cracked garden, and stream. Runoff hurried into the ditch. Some water seeped into soil. Roots drank it, leaves released some as vapor, and streams carried the rest away.

* * *

That night, Sora listened to drops dripping from the roof. “The water comes back,” she said.

Miri shook her head. “It changes places. That is why it is a journey.”

The next morning, the children marked their route on a cloth map. They placed a Sun at one corner, a cloud above the mountain, a stream down the slope, a hidden underground line, and a tiny glass near the village. Pip’s path could begin anywhere and join another path.

A new cloud gathered above the hill. Its droplets touched, grew, and fell as rain. Sora caught one in her hand.

“Hello, Pip,” she said, smiling.

The drop shone for a moment, then slipped between her fingers and returned to the earth.
