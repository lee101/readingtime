# The Star That Was Too Bright to Sleep

![Cover art: The Star That Was Too Bright to Sleep](../art/accessible/78-the-star-that-was-too-bright-to-sleep.webp)

A star came down into the school yard on a Friday and it would not stop shining.

“It is the size of a dinner plate,” said Nan. “And it is the middle of the afternoon.”

Ali was six. He stood with his blanket round his shoulders and put his hand over his eyes. He could still see it through his fingers.

“Is it stuck?” he said.

“It is only there,” said Nan.

“Can somebody turn it off?”

“No,” said Nan. “Nobody in this village has a handle on it. Come away, the tea is cold.”

“We tried the curtains,” said Mrs Bell. “Every house shut them, and every house left them open again, because a shut curtain is a lie.”

Then nobody slept. Not Ali, and not his father, and not the girls on the bridge.

* * *

“I have not slept since Friday,” said the man who brings the bread. “Nor has my wife.”

“We tried blankets,” said Mr Pryce. “We lay on the pavement with them over their heads. The light came in under the edges and sat in our eyes.”

Mr Pryce got a bucket.

“He is not being anything,” said Mrs Bell. “He is being a star, and I would like my sleep back.”

They put the bucket over him.

“It would not go over,” said Mr Pryce. “The star is too big for a bucket.”

So the bucket sat down on its own handle in the road and wobbled there like a bowl.

“A hymn,” said the parson. “In case a thing from up there answers to a hymn.”

They sang all four verses slowly, and the star got brighter the way a puppy does when you whistle at it. It was pleased. It was not being rude at all.

The men were cross about the hymn, and everybody was very polite.

* * *

Old Fen sat on the low wall by the well with his hat on his knees, and said nothing, as he had said nothing for some months.

Ali found him on Thursday morning, with his blanket round him.

“You are not talking,” said Ali.

“No,” said old Fen.

“They have all gone to bed by nine,” said the baker, outside his shut shop. “I have had two men asleep in my queue.”

“A star sleeps too,” said old Fen.

“How do we get it to sleep?” said Ali.

“Be tired,” said old Fen. “You cannot send a thing to sleep. You have to be tired yourself, and then it gets tired with you.”

* * *

So the village decided to be extremely tired.

“Go to bed,” said old Fen, at every door in the valley. “Go to bed and be extremely tired.”

“Curtains to,” said old Fen, going along the row. “And leave them open.”

They went to bed in the middle of the bright afternoon, and they did it politely, slowly, with a great deal of nodding.

At half past one the houses went quiet, and then, because it was the middle of a bright afternoon, the village slept.

The star slept with them.

It came down off the wall and hovered a foot above the hopscotch squares. It turned itself over, and it went out like a lamp somebody had put a cloth over, only a lamp with cloth on it.

The star snores. It snores light.

* * *

The sun came up at half past five, as it does, and the whole village got up in it.

Everybody had slept for four hours in the day and nobody in the night, and every person in the valley was slightly tanned, the parson and the man who brings the bread and old Fen, who had not gone down at all.

“The star is asleep,” said Ali.

It was not. It was on the wall again, shining, and it looked as if it had slept very well.

* * *

On Sunday they gave the star a name.

“Children first,” said the parson. “You have been keeping it company.”

They voted with their hands. There were six names on the board.

“It is a star,” said Mr Cooper. “A star is a bulb with a long way to go.”

“You may say that after,” said the parson.

When they came to Ali, he put his hand up and said one word. The hall counted it out loud. The star was called SIX, because that was the hour it got everybody up, and because Ali was six.

Mr Cooper put his hand down and packed his board away. The parson wrote SIX on the board, the whole hall said it out loud together, and nothing has ever been said about the other name since.
