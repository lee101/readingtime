# The Mirror with Nothing Left to Say
![Cover art: The Mirror with Nothing Left to Say](../art/accessible/21-the-mirror-with-nothing-left-to-say.webp)

The mirror had answered the same question for one hundred years.

Who is the fairest?

It had watched queens arrive in silk, princes in polished boots, and children with mud on their knees. It had compared every face with the face the queen wanted reflected.

The answer was always the same.

Then Snow White arrived.

She had a red cloak, dark hair, and a worried little mouth. She stood before the mirror after the household had gone quiet.

“Mirror, mirror,” she said. “Who is the fairest?”

The mirror did not answer.

At first, Snow White thought it was broken. Then she noticed the face in the silver surface. It was not showing her. It was showing the room behind her: a flour bowl, a dropped broom, and the queen standing in the doorway.

The queen’s hands tightened around her sleeves.

“Say it,” the queen whispered.

The mirror stayed silent.

* * *

The next morning, nobody knew what to do.

The cook had burned the porridge. She stood before the stove, listening for a bell that did not ring. The gardener had planted three rows of beans in the same place. The stable hand had polished the same brass buckle until it shone.

The huntsman stood by the door with his bow across his arms. He had not been outside.

“Why are we all quiet?” Snow White asked.

No one answered. The queen had forbidden the word *different*. The household obeyed before anyone had explained why.

Snow White picked up the broom. “I can sweep. Can someone show me where the flour spilled?”

The cook pointed to the floor. “There.”

The gardener watched the beans. “I planted them by mistake.”

“Then we can move some,” said Snow White.

They worked. The cook made a second pot of porridge. The gardener lifted each seedling with his spade. The huntsman carried water to the cool kitchen stones.

At noon, the mirror was still quiet.

The queen turned on it. “You have served this house.”

The mirror showed the queen’s tired face. It did not rank her.

“I will not be compared again,” said the mirror.

The queen’s lips trembled. “I need an answer.”

“You need a promise you can keep,” said the mirror.

* * *

That evening, the household gathered near the hearth.

They did not begin with the queen. They began with the cook.

“I am afraid of mistakes,” she said. “So I hide them in the ashes.”

The gardener looked at his hands. “I am afraid of bad harvest. So I plant the same seed until I am too tired to think.”

The huntsman lowered his bow. “I am afraid of the forest. I have told the queen that the woods are empty when they are not.”

The queen sat very still. “I am afraid of being forgotten. So I asked the mirror to make one face outrank every other face.”

The mirror listened. Snow White listened too.

“I thought being fairest would make me safe,” the queen said. “It only made everyone afraid.”

The huntsman continued. “I saw a fawn with a limp. I fed it. I told the queen it had run away.”

The room grew still.

“Did you see the other animals?” Snow White asked.

He nodded. “They were hiding near the old bridge. They need a quiet place to drink.”

The gardener picked up his pencil. “There is a pool behind the west wall. I can make a shallow path.”

The cook stood. “And I can save broth for the birds. The big dog can watch from the gate.”

The queen looked at the work waiting in the room. “What should I do?”

“Start with one true thing,” said Snow White.

* * *

The next morning, the queen opened the west wall gate.

The huntsman made a shallow path to the pool. The gardener planted reeds along the bank. The cook scattered seed for the birds. The dogs lay near the water, calm and warm.

Snow White carried a bucket with the queen. Neither woman asked the mirror for an answer.

At noon, the mirror caught the sunlight.

The queen looked at it. “Are you still there?”

“Yes,” said the mirror.

“Will you answer me now?”

“No,” said the mirror. “I have nothing false left to say.”

The queen nodded. She took off her heavy crown and set it on the table.

That day, she helped in the kitchen. She sorted bowls with Snow White. She did not tell the cook which bowl was finest. She did not ask the mirror to watch the room.

At night, the household ate together. The cook’s porridge was warm. The gardener showed everyone the new reeds at the pool. The huntsman told the story of the fawn without hiding the part where he had been afraid.

The mirror stood in the hall. It reflected every face. It ranked none of them.

Snow White stood before it at last.

“Who is the fairest?” she asked.

The mirror showed her red cloak, flour on one sleeve, and a smile.

Snow White answered for herself. “Someone who is here.”
