# The Frog Who Chose the Pond
![Cover art: The Frog Who Chose the Pond](../art/accessible/20-the-frog-who-chose-the-pond.webp)

The frog sat on a lily pad in the palace pond.

He was green, bright-eyed, and very pleased with himself. He had caught three midges, chased a dragonfly, and found a warm place beside a water lily.

A princess came to the pond with a red bucket. She knelt at the edge. Her sleeves were too long. Her golden crown slipped when she leaned down.

The frog watched from under a leaf.

The princess looked tired.

“Hello,” she said. “Are you the frog who is supposed to be kissed?”

The frog’s eyes opened wide. “I am a frog. I am not supposed to be kissed.”

The princess blinked. “That is not what the song says.”

“Songs are short,” said the frog. “They leave out the asking.”

He jumped to a higher pad. The princess reached after him. Her foot slipped on wet stone. The crown fell into the pond with a silver splash.

Before she could dive, the frog touched the crown with one cold foot.

Gold light ran through the water.

* * *

The princess became a frog.

The frog became a prince.

The princess-frog wore a crown that was much too heavy. She tried to hop, and the crown slid over her eyes. She tried to speak, but every word came out in a small croak.

The prince sat on a stone and stared at her.

“See what you have done?” he said.

The princess-frog jumped. “You touched my crown.”

“You leaned over my pond.”

“Your pond?”

The princess-frog gestured at the water. “I have lived here since winter. You may stay on the bank, but this is my water.”

The prince opened his mouth to argue. A green spark popped at the tip of his tongue. He swallowed it quickly.

“Ah,” he said. “The spell is making every choice sound like a claim.”

The princess-frog nodded. Her crown felt lighter for one small moment.

Behind them, palace bells rang. Guards called over the wall. The king had sent people to find the missing princess.

The princess-frog tried to answer, but only a faint croak came out. The prince tried to explain, but a burst of bubbles spilled from his mouth.

They had been changed by magic. More important, they had both been told what to become before anyone asked what they wanted.

* * *

The princess-frog pointed toward a hidden drain. Water rushed through it, carrying leaves into the garden.

“The king’s gardener will be angry if the pond is empty,” she said.

The prince could not answer in words. He drew a tiny picture in the wet mud with his finger. It showed leaves, a grate, and three round stones.

The princess-frog understood. She pushed the leaves away. The prince wedged the stones under the grate. Together, they made a small dam. Water spread across the lower garden instead of rushing away.

They worked until the sun was low. Then the prince found a hole in the garden wall. He crawled through. The princess-frog followed.

Behind the wall was a hill. A small cottage stood among lavender and beans. An old woman opened the door.

“Two frogs,” she said. “How will I tell which one is hungry?”

The princess-frog pointed to herself, then to the prince, then to the basket of bread.

The old woman laughed. She made two leaf plates.

“Friends eat first,” she said.

The prince looked at the princess-frog. She looked back. The gold light flickered, but neither one tried to change the other.

* * *

At dawn, the spell loosened its grip.

The princess-frog grew tall. Her crown fell beside the pond. The prince stood on two feet, wearing wet leaves and a very confused expression.

The old woman gave them bread, tea, and time.

When they returned to the palace, guards rushed to the gates. The king demanded an answer. The old queen stepped forward. She had listened at the cottage window.

“Your daughter was changed when the frog touched her crown,” she said. “The spell exchanged them without asking either one first.”

The king frowned. “A kiss should have changed the frog.”

“Then why did no one ask the frog to kiss?” said the princess.

The prince looked down. “I did not choose this shape. I did not choose palace life. I chose the pond.”

The princess touched her wet crown. “I do not want to live in a tower where every room is planned for me. I want a home near the water, with a gate I can open.”

The king looked at the pond. At last, he pointed to the hill. “Build there. The frog may keep the pond.”

The princess smiled. The old queen smiled too. Not everyone had chosen the same place, but everyone had answered for themselves.

* * *

The cottage beside the pond had two rooms and a broad window.

The prince helped clear the path. The princess planted beans beside the hill. The old woman made a third chair for visitors.

Sometimes, travelers asked about the old spell. The prince told them the true story. He never said a kiss had saved anyone. He said a frog had said no. A princess had said where she wanted to live. Then they had built a gate.

The pond stayed cool in summer. The beans climbed over the fence. At night, the princess sat by the water and listened to frogs sing.

One small frog sat on a lily pad nearby.

“Will you kiss me?” he called.

The princess leaned down, smiling. “No, thank you. But you may sit beside me.”

The little frog hopped closer.
