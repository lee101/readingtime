# The Lantern for a Lost Cloud

![Cover art: The Lantern for a Lost Cloud](../art/accessible/24-the-lantern-for-a-lost-cloud.webp)

Mina, who was seven, found the lantern behind the old rain barrel.

It was small enough to fit in both hands. Its brass body was cool and smooth, with a little glass door in front. Inside, a cloud slept on a bed of rainwater.

The cloud was no bigger than a bowl of cotton. It puffed and settled while Mina watched.

“Hello,” she whispered.

The cloud did not wake. Mina carried the lantern home, tucked against her blue coat. When she set it by the kitchen window, the cloud rolled over and sighed.

Mina’s grandmother looked at the lantern. “That is a storm lantern,” she said. “Clouds sleep inside until a gentle rain calls them.”

“But this one is sleeping very hard.”

Grandmother touched the glass. “It may have forgotten what rain sounds like. A cloud wakes only when rain touches every part of it.”

That night, Mina carried the lantern to bed. She placed it on her nightstand and listened to the rain tick against the window.

Tick.

Tick-tick.

The cloud stretched, but it did not wake. Instead, a small breeze slipped from the lantern. It puffed Mina’s blanket into a triangle and blew her slippers under the bed.

Mina laughed. “You are making weather.”

The cloud sneezed.

A raindrop landed on Mina’s nose. Then another landed on her pillow. Soon a damp circle spread across the floor. The lantern was making weather without any rain from the sky.

The next morning, Mina took the lantern to the school garden. A gentle sprinkler hissed among the beans. The cloud leaned toward it, but it stayed asleep.

Mina counted the sprinkles out loud. “One, two, three.”

The cloud gave a tiny blink.

Grandmother had said that a cloud wakes only when rain touches every part of it, not just a few drops. Mina looked at the darkening sky. The first drops fell as she carried the lantern home.

The cloud stirred with a soft rumble.

Mina set the lantern down beside the flowerpots. Rain spilled from inside it, though the sky was clear. It filled the flowerpots and washed a stripe of mud across the path. The laundry pole leaned over. A bucket floated by, full of water.

Mina chased the runaway bucket until it bumped into a garden gnome. The gnome wore a red hat and a surprised smile.

The weather keeper came at the end of the day. Her name was Mrs. Vale. She wore a long yellow raincoat and carried a silver rain gauge and a small chest. Her boots squished softly.

Mina showed her the lantern. Mrs. Vale crouched beside it and listened.

“The cloud is asleep,” Mina said. “It is trying to wake up, but it is doing it wrong.”

Mrs. Vale nodded. “A cloud wakes when rain touches every part of it. Too much rain at once can send it back into its sleep.”

They took the lantern to the hill above town. Mina turned on the old sprinkler. A fine silver arc of water rose into the air. The cloud floated up and down inside the glass, trying to follow each drop.

Mrs. Vale opened the small chest. Inside were ribbons of bright cloth. Mina tied red, gold, blue, and green ribbons to a stick. When the sprinkler made a rainbow in the mist, she held the stick beneath it.

The colors shimmered across the lantern. The cloud stretched toward them.

“Rain can be gentle,” Mrs. Vale said. “It can shine in many colors. It can fall safely.”

A thunderclap sounded from far away. The cloud shivered and tucked itself into the lantern.

Mina counted with her fingers. “One soft drop. Two soft drops. Three soft drops.”

She counted every drop she could see. Mrs. Vale counted with her, touching one finger for each splash in the garden. They did not count the thunder. Instead, they counted until the water fell steadily, without a storm.

One. Two. Three. Four.

Mina’s voice stayed calm. Mrs. Vale’s hands stayed steady. The rainbow shimmered above them, bright as a ribbon.

Then the cloud opened its silver eyes. It took one soft breath. The lantern filled with clear, warm rain. The cloud grew until it was the size of Mina’s head, but it stayed gentle. Mina opened the little glass door, and the cloud drifted out. Its rain dripped into the waiting soil, making the grass shine.

Mrs. Vale smiled. “It remembered.”

The cloud floated upward and made one perfect circle around the lantern. Then it slipped back inside. The sun came out. Across town, the school garden sparkled, and every flower stood a little taller.

That evening, Grandma made soup while Mina dried the lantern with a soft cloth. The cloud slept peacefully inside, no longer blowing or splashing. Outside, the night was quiet and safe after the rain.

Mina placed the lantern on her nightstand. Now and then, a little rain whispered against the window.

The cloud had found its rain, and Mina had found a new way to help. Whenever the sky felt dry, she carried the lantern outside and counted the gentle drops until the world smelled sweet and clean.
