# The Promise Under the Rainbow

![Cover art: The Promise Under the Rainbow](../art/accessible/34-the-promise-under-the-rainbow.webp)

The day the rainbow appeared, the whole town turned toward the river.

Niko was building a paper bridge for the school’s bridge contest. The rules were simple: make a bridge that can hold three toy cars, and bring it to the judging table before sunset. Niko had promised his sister, “I will finish it before the rainbow disappears.”

His bridge had a strong base, but one arch still needed a curve. He planned to add it in the afternoon.

“Mom, will you check the glue when you come home?” he asked.

His mother was packing for a market trip. “If the rain holds off.”

* * *

At lunch, Niko saw a rainbow over the school roof. He had only three hours before sunset. He hurried to his desk and bent close to the paper bridge. The arch balanced when he nudged it. Then a gust blew through the open window. The bridge slid from his hand, landed in a puddle of paint water, and softened.

For the first time, Niko did not feel the urge to hide the damage. He opened a fresh sheet and started again.

That was when a small girl fell in the courtyard.

Her name was Tami. She had an asthma pump in her pocket, but it was not there. She tried to speak and could only whisper. Tami’s brother ran for a grown-up while Niko knelt beside her. He did not know how long the medicine would take to arrive. He could keep her sitting upright, keep the other children back, and tell the teacher exactly what happened.

The school bell rang. Niko looked toward the classroom. His bridge was still on the desk.

* * *

The teacher came with the school nurse. Niko stayed with Tami until her pump arrived. A parent explained that Tami’s family had left the spare at home, and another child ran to fetch it. The nurse helped Tami breathe slowly. Niko counted with her: one for the blue sky, two for the clouds, three for the door.

When the pump finally worked, Tami’s brother gave Niko a soggy paper star. “You stayed.”

Niko looked at the clock. Two hours had gone. He could no longer finish the bridge before sunset. The rainbow had faded, and the promise he had made to his sister felt heavy in his chest.

His sister Lily found him by the office door. “You said you would finish.”

“I need to tell the teacher,” he said. “I can finish after the contest, but I will not say I did it before.”

Together they found Mrs. Vale. Niko explained the gust, the broken bridge, and Tami’s breathing emergency. He did not make the emergency sound like a small excuse.

Mrs. Vale looked at the contest rules. “The deadline is a real deadline. I cannot pretend otherwise. I can hold your place if you bring your plan and materials, and you can enter the repair session tomorrow morning.”

Niko nodded. “I’ll bring the paper, the glue, and an honest note.”

* * *

At sunset, he walked into the judging room carrying a tray with the ruined bridge, a new sheet of paper, and a note. The other contestants had placed their models on tables. Niko put his tray at the end.

Mrs. Vale read the note. “This bridge was damaged by wind, and I chose to help Tami. I did not finish before the deadline. I have brought the plan and materials for repair.”

A judge examined the new drawing. “You may set up your place now, but you cannot receive a prize today.”

“I understand,” Niko said.

He arranged the paper curve and tested it with one toy car. The arch held. He did not claim that it was finished, and he did not blame the wind. The judge marked a small line beside his name, not a score, but a promise to return.

* * *

The next morning, Niko came back with Lily for the separate design-only repair session, not the sunset bridge contest. They repaired the bridge together, adding two thin ribs beneath the arch. The three cars crossed, one after another. Mrs. Vale judged the repaired designs and awarded Niko second place for design, while the first-place ribbon went to the child who had repaired a bridge using only string.

Niko looked longer at the first-place ribbon than he had expected. Lily nudged his sleeve.

“You kept your promise,” she said.

“I kept the promise I made after the emergency,” Niko replied. “I told the truth about the first one.”

Outside, a new rainbow appeared over the river. This time Niko did not make a promise. He opened his notebook and wrote down the plan for a bridge they could build together, with enough time to listen if the world changed.
