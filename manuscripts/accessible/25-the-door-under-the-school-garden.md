# The Door Under the School Garden

![Cover art: The Door Under the School Garden](../art/accessible/25-the-door-under-the-school-garden.webp)

Elsie was six. She wore a yellow cardigan with one elbow patched by green thread, blue trousers, and red boots that left a flower-shaped print in the soil. She walked with her shoulders tucked in, as if the wind might get under her sleeves.

Ravi was six too. He wore a striped blue shirt, green shorts, and a cap pulled low over his curly hair. He skipped whenever he was pleased. He was pleased now, because a speck of blue had appeared beneath the school garden.

They found it behind a row of turnips. It was a tiny door, no taller than a jam jar. Its paint was the color of a clear morning sky. A brass knob shone at its middle, and a small square window reflected Elsie and Ravi like two very small giants.

Ravi crouched beside it. “Do you think a mouse lives there?”

Elsie touched the soil around the door. A tiny weed had curled around one hinge.

Before she could answer, the door gave a soft click. It opened by itself.

Behind it was not a mouse hole. It was a very small garden. Seed packets stood in neat rows. A blue watering can leaned against a white wall. Three little windows looked onto flowers with petals as round as spoons.

A voice came from inside. It sounded like a breeze moving through leaves.

“Helpful garden hands may enter,” it said. “Idle hands may only look.”

Elsie looked at her muddy hands. So did Ravi.

Elsie pulled the weed away from the hinge. Ravi used his fingertips to pat the loose soil around the door. The door swung wider, and the little garden inside became brighter.

On its tiny workbench lay a note made from a green leaf.

RETURN EACH TOOL TO ITS HOME BEFORE LATE SPRING REACHES THE PLAYGROUND SOIL.

Ravi read it slowly. His lips moved with every word.

Outside, a thin line of new green was creeping across the playground. It moved from the garden toward the sand, one small plant at a time. The blue paint on the door looked paler than before.

“The tools are not home,” Elsie said.

They hurried to the garden shed. Its green door hung crooked, and a picture on the wall showed every tool in its place. A watering can belonged on the left. A trowel belonged in a bucket. A hand fork belonged on a nail. A rake belonged beside the compost bin.

But the shed was empty.

The watering can was behind the bench. The trowel was under the bean leaves, where a ladybird sat on its handle. The hand fork was in the sandbox, buried beneath a smooth blanket of sand. Only the wooden rake could not be found.

Ravi pointed to the shed picture. “The rake is long. It might be by the fence.”

Elsie looked across the garden. The playground line of green had reached the corner nearest the gate. The tiny door’s paint faded toward gray.

They searched together. Elsie checked beneath the bench and between the flowerpots. Ravi looked along the fence, tapping the ground with his cap. They heard the little voice inside the door.

“Helpful hands, quick and kind.”

They followed the sound. It came from the sandbox. Ravi crawled on his hands and knees and scraped away sand with his fingers. At last, he pulled out the wooden rake. Its handle had a friendly red knob.

Elsie took one end. Ravi took the other.

“It is heavy,” Elsie said.

Ravi puffed out his cheeks. “We can carry it slow.”

They carried the rake between them, taking tiny steps so it would not bump the flowers. When they reached the shed, they set the rake beside the compost bin. The door’s blue paint brightened.

They returned the watering can, then the trowel, and finally the hand fork. Each tool went back to the place shown in the picture. The green line across the playground stopped moving.

When the last tool was home, the tiny blue door opened wider than before. Its garden swelled with green. Beans climbed up the white wall. Round red flowers opened beside the path.

The voice said, “Hands that help may always find me.”

Elsie and Ravi looked at the playground. The late-spring plants had not reached the sand after all. Instead, the hard soil beside it had softened. A ring of tiny leaves had appeared around the door, and each leaf shone in the sun.

The next day, they found the door again. It was smaller than Elsie’s boot, but the garden inside was wider than the shed. Elsie returned a dropped trowel. Ravi returned a watering can to its painted spot.

And the school garden grew greener all around them.
