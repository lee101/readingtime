# The Library Mouse and the Midnight Kite

![Cover art: The Library Mouse and the Midnight Kite](../art/accessible/16-the-library-mouse-and-the-midnight-kite.webp)

At midnight, when the library doors clicked shut, a paper kite shot through the stacks.

Nibble the mouse was returning a lost thimble when she heard a sharp *snap*. The kite bounced off two ladders and vanished behind the History of Buttons. A blue corner poked out beneath an atlas shelf.

Nibble hurried closer. A silver bookmark hung from the kite’s tail. It hummed, as important things did. Lost buttons did not hum. Only unfinished things did.

On one side of the bookmark was a picture of a blue door. On the other, in tiny letters, were the words:

**For Mara, who has a story but cannot find its first line.**

Beneath them was a chapter:

*Mara stood before the blue door. She had one key, three brave thoughts, and no promise the road would be kind. She put her hand on the latch.*

The chapter stopped there.

Nibble knew Mara. She was nine, with a dark braid and a blue notebook. Every Wednesday she read enormous books in the library, but her notebook stayed closed. Once, Nibble saw her write, *Once there was*, and stop. When Nibble offered a crumb, Mara had whispered, “What if the story is bad?”

“Stories can be quiet,” Nibble had answered. “They can take a long time to grow.”

Mara had looked down at the notebook, but she had not written again.

The bookmark tugged toward the Children’s Reading Room. Nibble climbed onto the kite and held the paper frame. “To Mara,” she said.

The kite flew between shelves that were rearranging themselves. Books slid sideways, wooden steps unfolded, and a ladder rolled after them like a friendly, too-tall dog. Nibble grabbed a shelf bracket as the kite dived into a tower of big, fat books.

A gust burst through a high window. The kite swung over a dark maze of index cards. A brass label flashed below: **RETURN TO THE READER WHO WAITS**.

Then the kite rolled sideways, and its tail snagged. Nibble pulled hard. The paper strained.

Aha. The kite was not lost, and no thief had stolen the bookmark. The midnight kite carried unfinished things to readers who could help finish them. But Mara’s name was hidden beneath a smear of ink, so the kite could not find her alone.

Nibble landed on a narrow rail and hurried to the Children’s Room.

Mara was not in her usual window seat. A folded blanket and a cup of cold tea sat there, but no girl. The blue notebook lay open. *Once there was* was crossed out. Below it, Mara had written, *What comes next?*

Nibble’s heart thumped. A page turned somewhere in the room.

She followed the sound past baskets of stuffed moons and a puppet dragon. At the base of the tall reading tree, she found a little hole in the wall. Cold air breathed through it, carrying the scent of dust and kite paper.

Beyond the hole was a passage made from forgotten books. The kite was tangled around a giant atlas, and Mara’s bookmark shone beneath one corner.

“Mara?” Nibble called.

A small voice answered, “Over here!”

Mara’s fingers appeared on the other side. Together they pulled. The atlas shifted, and the kite sprang free.

Then a stack leaned across the passage. The wooden ladder was gone. Nibble saw a beam of moonlight and the library balcony far above.

Mara climbed onto the kite while Nibble cut a loose thread from the atlas binding. The kite bucked, but the passage opened just long enough for Mara to squeeze through. Nibble followed with the silver bookmark tucked into her scarf.

They reached the balcony as the clock struck one.

The kite’s red tail thread pulled toward the locked window. It was a warning line. If they followed it, they would be trapped until the library opened.
Nibble followed the red thread with one paw. At its end she found a tiny brass hook beside the return slips. A slip marked MARA—BLUE NOTEBOOK—RETURN BY DAWN fluttered there, with a purple thumbprint at one corner. On the back, a blue arrow pointed east: **BALCONY ROUTE—FOLLOW THE RED THREAD.**

Nibble tucked the slip into her pocket. Now the warning line made sense. The library’s first bell would lock the shelves, and anything still flying after that would be lost in the morning delivery cart. The midnight kite had until dawn to bring an unfinished story home.

Nibble turned the slip over. The blue arrow pointed toward the east balcony, where the library’s kites waited for the first morning wind. The red thread pulled that way too. “We know the route now,” she said.

Mara looked at the red thread. “We can follow the gentle wind over the balcony, but the kite may tear.”

“Or we can untie the bookmark and lose our only way home,” Nibble said.

Mara held the kite’s frame. “The wind.”

Nibble tied the bookmark to its tail. “Together, then.”

The kite lifted them over the sleeping town. It passed the moon in the window and turned toward the Children’s Room. A sudden gust tore one paper corner loose. The silver bookmark slipped from the tail and fell toward the black branches of an oak tree.

Nibble could hold the kite and let the bookmark fall. Or she could let the kite go and chase it alone.

“Go!” Mara called. “I can steer this!”

Nibble released the frame. She landed on the balcony rail and hurried down the fire stairs. At the oak’s roots, she found the bookmark glowing beneath dead leaves. Beside it lay a tiny note: *If the reader cannot begin, begin for her.*

Nibble did not understand until she remembered the unfinished sentence. The story was not waiting for more ink. It was waiting for a voice.

She hurried back to the Children’s Room. The first gray light touched the windows. Mara stood with the kite hovering beside her.

“Read the chapter,” Nibble said.

Mara’s voice shook, but she read. “Mara stood before the blue door. She had one key, three brave thoughts, and no promise the road would be kind. She put her hand on the latch.”

She stopped. The bookmark shone.

“She opened it,” Mara said.

The new words appeared in the air, bright as dust in sunlight. They drifted into the open notebook and settled on its first page.

Mara read the last line. Then she picked up her pencil.

“What comes next?” Nibble asked.

“Next,” Mara said, “I write the beginning.”

On the page she wrote: *Once there was a mouse who knew the way home by the smell of rain.*

“That is a very good beginning,” Nibble said.

“It is only the first line,” Mara answered, smiling.

The bookmark clicked. A tiny silver bell appeared at its tip and rang once. The unfinished chapter was whole, and the midnight kite became still.

* * *

At sunrise, the library doors opened. Mara left her blue notebook on the seat. On the first page, beneath the story, was a new line: *When I am afraid, I will ask for help.*

Outside, the kite rose above the roof. Its red thread had become a ribbon of morning gold. It carried no unfinished words now. It carried a small paper door, folded from the last page of Mara’s new story.

Nibble watched it sail toward the blue sky. By breakfast, everyone in town would see a kite above the library and wonder what secret it carried.

Nibble already knew. Every brave beginning needs a little wind.

And sometimes, a small friend to hold the string.
