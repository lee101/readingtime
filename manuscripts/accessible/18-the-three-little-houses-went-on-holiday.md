# The Three Little Houses Went on Holiday
![Cover art: The Three Little Houses Went on Holiday](../art/accessible/18-the-three-little-houses-went-on-holiday.webp)

Every morning, the three houses compared themselves.

The straw house creaked in the wind. The wood house stretched its beams. The brick house stood tall and cool.

“We are the strongest,” said the brick house.

“We are the warmest,” said the wood house.

“We are the lightest,” said the straw house.

Their three little pigs lived inside. They hurried from room to room, carrying buckets, patching roofs, and answering the houses’ questions.

“We are strongest!”

“We are warmest!”

“We are lightest!”

By sunset, the pigs’ hooves were sore. The houses were still arguing.

Then a great wind shook a loose window. The straw house sighed. The wood house groaned. The brick house sent out a puff of dusty air.

The pigs stopped.

“You look tired,” said the smallest pig.

“We are,” said the straw house. “We have been standing in the same places since we were built.”

The wood house rapped a door. “People knock to see if we are sturdy.”

The brick house warmed its clean hearth. “People point at us to show how safe they are.”

“We have never had a holiday,” said the straw house.

The pigs looked at one another. Then all three squealed.

“On holiday!” they said.

* * *

The houses packed themselves.

The straw house rolled its thatch into a soft golden bundle. Its walls folded flat, tickling like dry grass.

The wood house clicked its shutters together. Its walls climbed down the nails in neat, light steps.

The brick house loosened three smooth red blocks. They rolled into a bag on little wooden wheels.

The pigs packed bread, apples, a wool blanket, and a pail of nails. Then they followed the houses along the river path.

At noon, the straw bundle stopped.

“I cannot go farther,” it said. “The path is hard.”

The wood house opened a little door. “Climb up. I can make a bridge from my loose boards.”

The brick house blocked the narrow path. “There is no room.”

The pigs worked together. They moved the red blocks into a low step. The wood house made a bridge over the wet stones. The straw house rested on top while the pigs pushed from behind.

By evening, everyone reached a hill above a lake. The houses unfolded beside a patch of soft grass.

“We are on holiday,” said the wood house.

“No building today!” cried the pigs.

“No measuring!” said the straw house.

“No comparing!” said the brick house.

The houses rested.

* * *

The next morning, the pigs woke to a crack.

The straw house had split from top to bottom.

“It is too dry,” it whispered. “Everything is too dry.”

The wood house creaked. A beam had split too.

The brick house was still solid, but its door would not open. “I am too full of cold. I cannot feel my hinges.”

The pigs had to work. They carried water from the lake. They made a cool shade over the brick house with leafy branches. They tied the straw house together with the wool blanket. The wood house held still while the smallest pig hammered its loose nails.

The houses tried to help. The straw house brushed cool water across the wood house. The wood house lifted a branch for the brick house. The brick house warmed a flat stone for breakfast.

Soon all three houses rested again. The smallest pig found a red flower.

“Holiday does not mean nothing is wrong,” it said. “It means we help when the work is real.”

The houses thought about that.

* * *

On the way home, the village had a surprise.

The hill path had washed into a deep ditch. One traveler had built a tiny bridge. It was strong for the bridge, but narrow for anyone carrying a basket. The houses had to stop.

The villagers gathered. They pointed at the straw house’s light bundle and the wood house’s neat boards. They pointed at the brick house’s strong blocks.

“Use the straw first,” someone said.

“No, use the wood.”

“The brick house can make the whole wall.”

The houses tightened.

The pigs stood between them. The smallest pig picked up a red block. The middle pig gathered two loose boards. The largest pig took a long piece of rope.

They did not compete. The brick house made a low retaining wall. The wood house laid steps across the ditch. The straw house made a soft rope for travelers’ hands. The pigs filled the gaps with stones and packed earth.

The path opened.

The houses folded themselves again. As they reached the village, they passed a little spring. The straw house made a shade over its seeds. The wood house carried a cool plank. The brick house warmed a small stone for the next house to rest on.

That night, the pigs cleaned the three rooms. They did not answer every creak and sigh.

One house asked, “Am I warm enough?”

“Yes,” said the middle pig, checking the window.

Another asked, “Is my roof secure?”

“Not yet,” said the smallest pig. “But we can fix it together tomorrow.”

The houses settled. Their doors stayed still until morning. The sun moved across the village, but none of them hurried to be the first house in the sky.
