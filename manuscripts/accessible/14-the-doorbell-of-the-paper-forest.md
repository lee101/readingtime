# The Doorbell of the Paper Forest

![Cover art: The Doorbell of the Paper Forest](../art/accessible/14-the-doorbell-of-the-paper-forest.webp)

“Ding!”

The tiny bell rolled across Nia’s bedroom floor.

It was no bigger than a strawberry. Its red button bounced on the rug, and the bell chased it toward the closet.

“Wait for me!”

Nia crawled after it. She pushed aside her school bag and a castle made from socks. The bell slipped under the bed and gave another cheerful *ding*.

Behind Nia’s bed, a curtain she had never noticed stirred. A narrow crack opened in the wall. Cold air smelled like dust, rain, and crayons.

The bell rolled into the crack.

Nia grabbed her coat and slipped through.

On the other side was a forest.

Every tree had a paper trunk. Crinkled leaves whispered overhead. A brown owl spread wings stamped *THE WISE OWL OF GINGERBREAD WOOD*. A long dragon made of blue lines curled around a silver moon. Between their branches, discarded story pages fluttered like moths.

The strawberry-sized bell waited on a curled page.

“You rang from my house,” Nia said.

“Ding!” it said.

“That means yes?”

“Ding!”

A path of blue writing led between the trees. Nia followed it. Soon, the marks became a map of the woods she knew. She even passed a neat blue P for post office.

Then a gust shook the branches.

Dozens of loose pages lifted into the air. They raced past her, smelling of dust and old glue. When they landed, the path had changed. A dragon was standing beside a castle. A whale sailed above a teacup. The blue P had become part of a dragon’s wing.

Nia wrapped both hands around her coat.

“I need to go home.”

The forest rustled. It sounded like hundreds of little pages clearing their throats.

Nia searched her coat pocket. Out came her folded walking map. It was covered in bus routes and corner shops. At home, she had folded the top corner to mark the short way back to the library.

She almost opened the map. Then she remembered how soft the paper trees were. A sharp wind could crumple them. She did not want to flatten the whole forest.

Instead, Nia stood the map up like a little tent. The folds rose like walls. Sunlight passed through them, and the creases made dark lines across the ground.

“Hmm.” Nia looked from one line to the next.

The lines pointed nowhere. The walking map was not a map of this forest. A dragon had sat on the bus stop.

The bell gave a soft ding.

Above them, the page moth swarm began to circle. The story trees leaned closer. Their branches made scratchy, rustling sounds.

Nia hugged the standing map. “You help too. You can keep the straight parts.”

She spoke to the paper. The words felt strange, but the map answered with a soft *fwip*. Its folded corner sprang toward the west.

The bell went that way, too.

Nia followed. She held the map upright and watched the folds bounce over roots and stones. The path grew narrow. Tall pages brushed her sleeves. An old sea story wrapped itself around her feet, but the bell pulled free.

Behind her, the forest changed. The dragon became a rowboat. The castle became a cup of soup. Every change made the paper trees sigh.

“Stay where you are,” Nia told them. “I only need to pass.”

The trees rustled in what might have been agreement.

At the edge of a clearing, Nia heard a sound.

Beep-beep-BEEP.

She knew it at once. That was the little horn on Dad’s blue bicycle. He used it when he turned corners or called her home from the garden.

But the sound was far away and thin, as if trapped inside a folded page.

Nia lifted the tiny bell.

“Can you help?”

The bell rang: “Ding!”

The papers trembled. The bee-beep-BEEP came again, this time from a stack of loose pages. Nia hurried over and lifted them carefully, one by one. Under the last page was a narrow opening. Beyond it she could see the green hedge behind her house.

The lost sound was leaking through the gap.

Nia could have crawled straight home. Instead, she held up the walking map. The wind caught its bus routes and corner shops. The maps and papers around her rose into a fluttering circle. For a moment, all the wind-tossed stories shook themselves at once.

A dragon rustled. A whale sneezed a raindrop. The wise owl cleared its throat and folded itself into a paper boat.

The trees relaxed. Their trunks stood straight. The loose pages settled gently on the path.

Nia’s map lay smooth and open, held by the little bell. Its folded corner pointed home.

“Come on,” she said.

She carried the map like a door through the last trees. The bell went first with a final *ding*. Nia crawled through the opening, pulled the curtain closed behind her, and rolled under her bed.

Dad’s blue bicycle horn went *beep-beep-BEEP* in the garden.

“Here!” Nia called.

The strawberry-sized bell sat up in her palm. Its red button shone beneath the bedroom light.

Outside, the wind moved the leaves. Somewhere below the old wall, pages rustled without breaking.

Nia smiled and gave the little bell a place on her shelf. It sat beside her walking map, folded just right.
