# World: The Ironwood Enclave

Nettlefold is a village of two hundred people who spend their lives carrying light across a dark forest floor: a lamplit lane of lamplighters, an oil foundry, a scriptorium, a hundred chimneys, and a hundred and one windows that must be counted every single night. Outside the lane, the Walking Wood stands up out of the ground at dusk and moves, forty feet of iron-barked tree on slow root-fists, so vast that villagers learn to call individual trees by name the way city children never do. For four hundred years a pact has held the border: the village keeps the lamps burning, the trees keep the boundary. This winter the sap on the pact-board is drying out in patches, the lamps are being dimmed to save oil, and the first ironwood has stepped over a boundary marker in daylight without being stopped.

## Setting

Nettlefold sits in a cold hollow of the Walking Wood, on a ridge of gravel where the trees cannot root comfortably even by day. The climate is wet, northern, and honest about itself: grey-green rain, a hard blue frost each morning, a light like watered pewter until the lamps come on at dusk. Buildings are timber-framed, low-roofed, shingled in split ironbark, with warm shuttered windows and a smell everywhere of hot oil, wet wool, and resin scraped from the lamplighters' coats. The one road in and one road out is Lamplighter Row, a stone-and-plank lane lit at eleven paces by iron-framed oil lamps, each with a chalked number and a little hinged door the wick-watcher must open twice a night.

Daily life is a schedule of small rounds. Oil is rendered at the Cindermill from rendered tallow and one spoon of ironwood sap per lamp per season, which everybody resents and nobody refuses. The foundry smelts bog iron into nails, hinges, drip-pans and lamp-frames, and its smiths work by ear, hammering to a count because a loud forge indoors carries. The Mere of Slow Water lies north, and no ironwood crosses it. Food is smoked eel, sow-bread, mushroom ragout, and a bitter green tea brewed from boiled bark that everyone pretends to like. Market day is Thursday at the Nettlefold cross: sacks of peat, bundles of lamp wick, three sellers of lamp oil, one illegal seller of ironwood charcoal, a rotating argument about the border, and a queue at the notice-slate where the Warden's orders are nailed up in public so that everyone can be angry at them on schedule.

## The Magic

1. **Ironwood roots by day and walks by night.** A standing ironwood is rooted only while light is on it. At dusk it lifts, and in the dark it moves — always slowly, always heavily, always downslope or toward water.
2. **The pact is a document, not a spell.** Its text is birch-bark written in ironwood sap and sealed with a nail of cold iron. It holds because it is read, aloud, by two parties, every Samstag night. An unread line goes void; a void line is not struck out, it simply stops being true.
3. **Lamplight is presence, not fire.** A lamp burns as long as there is oil and a wick-watcher who is awake and intending it. A lamp lit by a hurrying hand gutters. A lamp lit by nobody, or by somebody who has stopped paying attention to the living, goes out.
4. **Ironwood heartwood is dead.** The tree is a shell shaped like a tree. It does not speak. What sounds like the Walking Wood talking is real birdsong, cut short — the forest's birds have always known better than to finish a phrase.
5. **Named log, held path.** Cut and bound with iron and given a name aloud, a felled ironwood will hold one path open and keep it. A log held without a name is only shelter; it shelters wolves, mud, and rot, and it starves.
6. **The cost of the border is light itself.** Every lamp is paid for with a spoon of sap a season, and sap is the trees' living substance. A well-lit border is a well-fed border, and feeding it costs the wood. This is the reason the village wants fewer lamps.
7. **The limit: slow water.** Ironwood may not cross running or slow water. Wading, a single ironwood manages a lumbering four steps and loses the tread; a full crossing needs a rootbridge or a boat and a prayer.
8. **Ironwood cannot be cut, burned, or killed without dying inside it.** A felled ironwood lies down, and its shadow lies down with it, and the ground under it will not grow bracken for seven years. This is why nobody has ever taken a tree in anger, and why the charcoal seller at the cross is a problem, not a villain.
9. **No living hand may be a signature.** Blood is forgery and is not read. The Wood accepts sap and cold iron, and it accepts them once.
10. **The border is the light, not the trees.** Where lamps end, the Wood does not stop so much as forget where it was supposed to stop. The line moves inward by whatever distance the darkness was.

## Cast

**Nessa Brack**, 14, lamplighter's apprentice, third season. *Want:* to be signed off as a full wick-watcher before the first frost. *Flaw:* she quietly takes other people's shifts and other people's debts and calls it nothing. *Detail:* two fingers of her left hand stained permanently green-black with sap, and she does not hide them. *Speech habit:* ends sentences with a small flat "so it goes."

**Oren Vall**, 62, Warden of the Wards, the man who nails up the orders. *Want:* to keep the last promise he made to his sister, who asked to be buried under a lampless stone. *Flaw:* he hoards small comforts in a locked tin, one at a time, and cannot say why. *Detail:* a brass-capped ash walking staff worn to a shine on the left side, and a left knee that clicks audibly on the stairs. *Speech habit:* "Aye. Aye, but—" and then a pause that lasts too long.

**Merrin Vale**, 9, a lamplighter's daughter, the only child in Nettlefold allowed to be indoors during the reading. *Want:* to see the birdsong whole, all of it, every morning. *Flaw:* she believes anything she is told, out loud, by anyone she loves. *Detail:* a red knitted cap with earflaps, worn year-round, with a hole she has knitted closed four times. *Speech habit:* asks "is that allowed?" before anything she wants.

**Tace Aldren**, 19, founder's apprentice at the Cindermill, third generation. *Want:* to make a nail good enough that his grandmother's name is not on the tally any more. *Flaw:* contempt for the pact as an old and unmodern thing, and a private delight in being able to break it. *Detail:* a burn-scarred inner forearm she does not cover, and brass forge goggles pushed up into untidy hair. *Speech habit:* swears in trades — "draw-filing hell," "slag luck."

**Elder Sable Ashdown**, 74, keeper of the Scriptorium and the only living hand that can read the pact aloud. *Want:* to reach the last page of the board before her sight goes. *Flaw:* she has been tidying it, and tidying is a kind of editing. *Detail:* a beard fine as lichen, three strands of it silver-leafed, and one clouded eye. *Speech habit:* reads aloud in a soft sing-song, then stops and repeats the line, slower.

**Sixpence**, four years old, a hooded crow. *Want:* to be answered. *Flaw:* a mimic; he repeats calls without ever having been taught what they mean. *Detail:* missing the top third of his left beak, so his calls come out with a whistle in them. *Speech habit:* repeats any two-word phrase he likes, especially "lights, then," and "count it."

## Places

**Lamplighter Row.** The one lit street: a gravel-and-plank lane between shuttered houses, eleven paces between iron-framed oil lamps, each with a chalked number, a hinged little door, and a brass plate listing the wick-watchers of that stretch. At dusk you can stand at the top of it and see the whole lane at once, a string of small warm circles laid down on the wet ground, and the dark pressing in from both sides like water.

**Nettlefold Cross.** The market square, a bowl of beaten gravel with a standing stone at the middle and the notice-slate on a post beside it. Thursday fills it with peat sacks, eel barrels, oil churns, three gossiping knots of weavers, and the charcoal seller with his cart too far east of the boundary line. Order is nailed up here so that everyone can argue with it in public.

**The Cindermill.** A long low forge shed with three chimneys, a great hood of black soot, and a waterwheel that turns only when the stream is high enough. Inside: eight anvils, a row of bellows, and the wet cold-iron smell that gets into wool and stays for years. The light in here is orange and jumping, and it is the only light in Nettlefold that is not a promise to anybody.

**The Scriptorium.** A cold upper room over the tithe store, one north window, a wall of birch-bark leaves on oak boards, and an oak reading stand with two worn hand-grips. The pact-board fills one wall, forty-one lines of sap script with a cold-iron nail through the last line, and a grey rectangle where somebody has used a knife on the wood.

**The Barren Ring.** A bare hill of chalk and gorse, two miles east, where the lamplight has never reached: no trees at all, just thin grass and the ruined walls of an older village, hollow. From the top, on a clear night, you can see the whole dark wood standing up and moving at once, and the fringe of your own small lights on its near edge, and how very much darker it gets past them.

**The Mere of Slow Water.** A wide, flat, peat-brown lake north of the enclave, glassy in the morning and pewter at dusk, fringed with reed and drowned willow. It is the one border the Wood cannot argue with. Children are taught to swim there before they are taught to read a lamp number.

## Visual Style

A cold northern woodland palette of wet slate, iron-bark grey-violet, lichen green-black, and the two warm accents that matter: sap amber and lamp gold. Lighting is dusk and pre-dawn, low blue ambient with one small hand-held flame carving a hot circle out of it, and the frequent suggestion of a second set of lights much further away and much dimmer. Materials are split ironbark shingles, black-wet gravel, cold hammered iron, brass lamp fittings, resin, smoke, and oiled wool. The era reads late-medieval northern and hand-built: felt hats, oil lamps, no steam, no glass towers, no armour that glitters. The single recurring motif, reused across every cover in this world, is one small lit lamp held low in a vast dark wood, its light doubled in standing water.

## Motifs

1. **Sixpence on a lamp-post**, wings folded, head cocked, waiting to be answered.
2. **The cold-iron nail** — square-headed, black, and never warm.
3. **The tally of lit windows**, chalked on a slate at the top of the Row and checked twice nightly.
4. **A curl of birch bark with sap script on it**, the letters glossy, readable, and slightly crawling.
5. **The edge of light**, where gravel becomes bracken and the two grounds meet in a visible line.

## Thematic Spine

All nine stories are about the moment an arrangement everyone inherited stops being a thing you can simply live inside and becomes a thing you have to keep saying out loud, and about what happens to the people who say it. The accessible band treats that as a matter of attention and kindness: lamps, birds, a child learning a walk, the ordinary discipline of showing up. The intermediate band treats it as a matter of obligation and consequence — the dead who signed before you, the villages outside your light, the message you agreed to carry. The advanced band treats it as a matter of authorship: who holds the pen, who is permitted a name on the page, and whether a good-faith edit is still a betrayal.
