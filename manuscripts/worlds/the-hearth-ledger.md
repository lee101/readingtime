# World: The Hearth Ledger

Coldhay is a market town of seven holdings standing just inside its boundary, and the story everybody tells about them involves a mirror, an apple, and a woman who was too good to survive seven small houses. What the town tells it as a story is a story about vanity. What the boundary rolls say is a story about covenants. In Coldhay a word spoken under a roof with a witness binds the speaker's holdings in that house for a year, and an apple is a delivery, and a delivery takes effect in the body of the person who eats it and not in the mind, and no covenant can be made with a sleeping person, which is the single most important rule in the town and the one the visitors break every time.

## Setting

Coldhay sits where the Fosse way crosses the Ash, a compact market town of grey limestone, one weekly market on the cattle market, a wool yard, a quarry, and seven small holdings along the north boundary called the Seven Stands — seven houses in a row with their backs to the town, each holding a strip of land, each a separate legal house with its own hearth, its own door, and its own witness. A house in Coldhay is a house with a hearth in it, and the hearth is the thing that makes the house, and the seven are the only houses in town with a claim on the boundary itself.

The market is on Monday. The rolls are kept in the chapel above the tithe barn, and the rolls are the most carefully kept documents in the county, because Coldhay is a town where a document has a physical effect and everybody has learned that the hard way. Food is beef, a coarse sheep's cheese, barley, a good dark bread, and cider in quantity. The town is cold in winter in a way that is not weather but architecture, and the seven houses have no corridor and no spare room, and this is understood by everybody as a fact about character.

## The Magic

1. **A word spoken under a roof with a witness is a covenant,** and it binds the speaker's holdings in that house for one year from the day. It does not require an oath, a signature, or a ceremony. A witness is a person who is awake, of an age to know what is being said, and not one of the two parties.
2. **Consent given under a covenant is a term of that covenant,** and a term can be withdrawn only by the person it was given to, in the same room, alone, unprompted, and out loud. A withdrawal asked for, hinted at, or obtained in company is not a withdrawal.
3. **An apple is a delivery.** What is delivered is a term of a covenant, and it takes effect in the body of the eater, on eating, and it is not a matter of mind or opinion or belief, and it cannot be argued with afterwards.
4. **A sleeper is not a witness.** Nothing said to a sleeping person is a covenant, nothing said by a sleeping person binds anybody, and nothing done in a sleeping house is heard by the house. This is the rule that a whole town of competent people forgot.
5. **The glass is a record, not an oracle.** It does not answer. It reads back what has been said in that house, and the reading is the only true account, and it is exactly as complete and no more complete than the hand that kept it.
6. **A house of seven holders may be called.** When it is called, all seven must come, and each must answer for their own holdings, and one may not answer for another, on any grounds, including love.
7. **A covenant is discharged by performance, by expiry, or by the lender accepting less.** The third requires the lender to say so out loud, in front of a witness, in their own house, and it is the single most dangerous act available to a person in Coldhay, and it is done about four times a century.
8. **A foundling holds nothing and is owed by everybody.** A gift to a foundling is the only gift in Coldhay that cannot become a covenant, because there is nothing to encumber, and this is the reason foundlings are worth a great deal to people with debts.
9. **A woman who is asked seven times and answers never is not being polite.** She is holding a covenant open by refusing to complete it, and an open covenant accrues, and the accrual is the danger.
10. **Glass keeps what it is given and a record is only as good as the hand that kept it.** A broken mirror is a loss and can be replaced. A rewritten one is a felony, and is the only felony in Coldhay, and has been committed twice.

## Cast

**Wren Sallow**, 11, a foundling on the north boundary, three times found and twice gone, currently under the protection of a covenant made in a different house. *Want:* to be given a thing that is not a claim. *Flaw:* she has learned to refuse gifts, and refusing a gift in Coldhay is a declaration that can be entered, and she has made three of them. *Detail:* a grey felt hat turned up twice, and the habit of turning it up again when an adult looks at her. *Speech habit:* asks "who is it for" about every object, word, and kindness.

**Ivo Colm**, 55, the boundary clerk, thirty-one years in the chapel rolls, the only man alive who can read the 1902 hand. *Want:* to be asked to retire and be allowed to go on coming in. *Flaw:* he has a disagreement with the 1978 hand and has decided in advance that the 1978 hand is wrong, and it is not wrong, and he will find that out in the worst possible week of his life. *Detail:* a green reading arm bolted to the desk, and a cataract-yellow eye he reads around rather than with. *Speech habit:* quotes the previous entry exactly and then waits, as if it might finish itself.

**Halla Dree**, 34, holder of the second stand, who has asked the same question in four houses in one year and has had four different answers and is now, herself, part of somebody else's covenant. *Want:* to hear the question asked once more and answer it properly. *Flaw:* she has stopped counting which of the four she was in, and the bookkeeping of that is more complicated than the bookkeeping of the covenants. *Detail:* a hearthstone in her apron pocket, worn smooth, and a habit of standing with her back to her own wall. *Speech habit:* asks questions in the exact words she was asked, and waits for the exact answer.

**Tam Kelder**, 47, the youngest of the seven, twenty-two years in the third stand, and the only one of the seven who can read. *Want:* for the other six to stop. *Flaw:* he has kept a private book of what each of the six has said and under what witness, and has never used it, and has now been asked by two people to use it. *Detail:* a small hand-bound book kept inside a coat lining, and a habit of counting the doorways he comes through. *Speech habit:* narrates the covenant as it is written, word by word, and cannot be stopped without becoming unpleasant.

**Old Brannic Sallow**, 71, of the first stand, who has outlived four covenants and has been asked the question seven times and has answered never, and is therefore the most dangerous person in Coldhay and is only dangerous because of arithmetic. *Want:* to be asked, one more time, in front of a witness, and to be able to say the words. *Flaw:* he started the accumulation deliberately, years ago, because he did not like the man asking, and has been unable to stop since. *Detail:* a fire that is never let go out, day or night, in every photograph of him. *Speech habit:* "That is not an answer" and then nothing, for a very long time.

**Mira Ottery**, 9, who sleeps in the fifth stand four nights a week, and who has therefore been the reason a great deal happened, and does not know it, and whose family knows entirely. *Want:* to know why nobody wakes her up. *Flaw:* she has begun waking up on purpose, in rooms, at consequential moments, having no idea what she is interrupting. *Detail:* a habit of arriving silently in doorways, and a nightdress in a house with no corridor. *Speech habit:* asks questions at 4 a.m. in a very clear voice.

## Places

**The Seven Stands.** A row of seven limestone houses with their backs to the town and their fronts to the boundary, no corridor, no spare room, one hearth each, one door each. They are the only houses in Coldhay that can be called as a house.

**The Tithe Barn.** The roll room is above it: one chamber, one window, four hundred years of boundary rolls in a press, and a desk with a brass arm. Cold, bright, silent, and the single most dangerous room in the town.

**The Sleeping Room.** The back room of the fifth stand, where a nine-year-old sleeps four nights a week under a covenant made in a house she does not live in, with a door that does not lock, because a door that locks implies somebody might come in, and no covenant in Coldhay permits that.

**The Kitchen of the Second Stand.** The warmest room in Coldhay and the site of four covenants in one year. A long scrubbed table, a drying rack, a hearth with a stone in it, and a chair nobody will sit in because of what was said in it in 1971.

**The Boundary Wall.** Nine feet of limestone between the market and the pasture, with a coping stone, and seven gates. You cannot see over it and you cannot hear over it and both of these facts are load-bearing in Coldhay's law.

**The Market.** Monday, cattle and cheese and cider, six stalls under awnings, and the one place where all seven stands have to appear together in daylight, in public, and cannot speak.

## Visual Style

A palette of limestone grey, hearth orange, and wet pasture green, lit by a low fire inside a small dark house and a high cold sun outside it, so that every scene is a hard cut between the two. Materials: blackened soot above a lintel, scrubbing stone, wool, a pressed wool coat, a great deal of paper four hundred years old, a brass reading arm, a locked press, cold beef, and the nine-foot wall. The era reads 1860s-1900s, pre-electric: a gas bracket in the market, a bicycle against the wall, a handcart, oil in the passages. The recurring motif is the hearthstone — a single worn grey stone carried in an apron pocket, taken from a house and put in another, which is the oldest legal move in Coldhay and nobody will admit to having made it.

## Motifs

1. **The hearthstone** — one smooth grey stone, carried in a pocket, moved from house to house, and never asked after.
2. **The witness's two hands** — somebody awake, in the room, with both hands visible, which is how you tell a witness from a person who is merely present.
3. **The four-hundred-year roll** — a bound volume in a press, a folded signature, a hand that changes and no explanation.
4. **The glass that reads back** — a mirror, a window, a pane of a door, and the discovery that the surface has been the only honest party in the house for a century.
5. **The fire that is never let out** — Brannic Sallow's hearth, photographed in every census, lit in every weather, the surest sign of an open covenant.

## Thematic Spine

All nine stories are about a promise that holds because two people were awake, and about the arithmetic of refusing to speak. The accessible band treats it as a house: a gift, seven doors, a sleeping room. The intermediate band treats it as a document: a mirror, a gift, a covenant. The advanced band treats it as a roll: a collation, a private book, a discharge entered four centuries late.
