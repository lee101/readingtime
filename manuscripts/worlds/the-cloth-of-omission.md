# World: The Cloth of Omission

Sparable is a hill town of weavers whose looms do not weave cloth. They weave omissions, and the finest thing this town can make is a bolt on which something has been deliberately left out — a colour, a stripe, a figure, a border, a whole sleeve — and the value of the bolt is in what the weaver chose to deny it. The Emperor's New Clothes is remembered here as a scandal the town tells about itself, and the town's version has the tailors right and the Emperor wrong, because the child was not being clever. The child was the only person at the inspection who had not been taught what to leave out. Everything else in Sparable, from the Great Hall to the smallest pair of socks, is a regulation cloth: complete, honest, heavy, and worth nothing at all.

## Setting

Sparable stands on a dry chalk shoulder above the Wen, with the weaving sheds on the sunny side and the guildhall and the tanneries on the cold side, and the whole town organised around one question that nobody in it can answer: what is this bolt missing. The looms are long, low, shuttered rooms with the light kept off the warp; the weavers work in the half-dark, and the town's daylight is only for dyers, menders, and the inspection. Wool from the downland, linen from the river flats, silk for the three families who can afford it, and alpaca in one shed that has not been able to sell a metre in eleven years because the alpaca had nothing to omit.

The trade is loud in the way that a town of looms is loud, and then goes silent four times a day, and the silences are when the town finds out what it has agreed not to notice. The one room that is never warped is the Great Hall, hung with the Nine Omissions — the town's founding weave, of which eight survive — and it is the only building in Sparable with nothing missing from it, because it is a room rather than a cloth, and rooms do not work this way, and everybody finds that excuse extremely convenient.

## The Magic

1. **A loom weaves omissions.** A thread on this loom is a thing deliberately not included. A weave with one deliberate omission in it is worth more than a weave without, and the value is not proportional; it is enormous and it is entirely up to the weaver.
2. **A thing left out on purpose by two people who agree is gone, permanently, for everybody.** This is a weave of two: the weaver and the buyer, each holding one thread, each able to stop the other. It is the only irreversible thing in Sparable and the guild allows about four a year.
3. **A thing left out by one person alone is invisible only to that person,** who can always see exactly what they have hidden, which is why weavers are not permitted to attend their own daughters' weddings in a room they have worked in.
4. **A garment woven on omission cannot be described.** Any description of it supplies the thing it left out, and the description kills it. A commission must be written in the negative, all the way down, and the negative is the only contract this town honours.
5. **A complete cloth is a regulation cloth:** heavy, honest, and worth nothing. It is the law, it is what the assay says, and it is what every shop in Sparable sells.
6. **The loom reads the weaver.** Cloth woven by a person who does not believe in the omission comes off the loom complete, and the weaver will be ruined and the cloth will be regulation and both of these facts will be true at once.
7. **A child who has not been taught what to leave out sees the whole cloth.** This is why children are welcome at the inspection, and why the guild has never succeeded in making an adult see one.
8. **A commissioner seen wearing nothing has not been made naked.** He has been made unmentioned, and the city must go on exactly as it is, and a person who is unmentioned continues to be legally present and cannot be pointed at.
9. **An omission that is discovered cannot be reinstated.** It can only be left empty, which is a different and permanent thing, and the guild has a name for the change: it is called a gap, and there are four gaps in Sparable and one confession.
10. **The Nine Omissions are the true cloth of the town.** The town is what it has agreed not to notice, and the eighth survives, the ninth does not, and everything in between has been argued about for two hundred years.

## Cast

**Lysa Ferrant**, 17, the youngest weaver with a commission and the only one in the town who cannot stop omitting on purpose. *Want:* to be allowed to weave something her mother did not. *Flaw:* she has begun leaving out the parts of cloths that people rely on — a selvedge, a reinforcement, a name — and nobody has caught her yet. *Detail:* a shuttle burn across the pad of her right hand, and a habit of turning a finished bolt face-down before she looks at it. *Speech habit:* describes people by what she has decided not to include in them.

**Old Mab Ostry**, 71, a weaver, forty-nine years at one loom, blind in one eye and entirely unbothered. *Want:* to be asked to mend the eighth before she dies, which nobody permits. *Flaw:* she has woven nine omissions and remembers eight of them and has lied about the ninth for fifty years. *Detail:* a yarn-walker's notebook with knots in it, and a habit of counting looms by touch on the way to her own. *Speech habit:* answers a question about what she made by saying what she left out, and refuses, cheerfully, to say anything else.

**Corin Slee**, 34, assay-clerk for the guild, the man who stamps the word *complete* on four hundred bolts a year and cannot now remember the last one that passed him. *Want:* to be given a bolt he can honestly fail. *Flaw:* he has begun to suspect he has been failing the right cloths for years and has said so to nobody, including himself, in writing. *Detail:* a brass stamp on a lanyard he never puts down, and a habit of aligning the edges of a bolt before he looks at it. *Speech habit:* gives a number, a tolerance, and a recommendation, in that order, whatever is asked.

**Ilse Barrowe**, 9, the child of the inspection, who can see every cloth in Sparable entire. *Want:* to be allowed to say what she sees. *Flaw:* she has said it, once, at a funeral, and it was true, and it had to be given up as a gap, and she has not entirely forgiven anybody. *Detail:* a green ribbon on her wrist that is the guild's mark of an authorised child-inspector and which she treats as a joke. *Speech habit:* describes things exhaustively and cannot be interrupted, and when she is quiet she is choosing not to describe.

**Vaughn Ostry**, 46, the guild's commoner, a large warm unhurried man who has run the Great Hall commission for eleven years and has never been given a cloth he could describe. *Want:* to know what he has been wearing. *Flaw:* he has never asked, because a commissioner who asks is a commissioner who is told, and he is fond of his position. *Detail:* a collar he has had altered four times by four different tailors, none of whom asked why. *Speech habit:* says "I take it" and never "I see", and cannot explain the difference even when asked twice.

**Wenna Pike**, 27, a dyer, the only person in Sparable who works in daylight and therefore the only one who can see a bolt as a colour rather than a set of decisions. *Want:* to have her colours noticed. *Flaw:* she has been dyeing to a specification written in the negative for nine years and cannot now dye anything she likes, and has stopped knowing what she likes. *Detail:* permanently indigo to the second joint, and a habit of naming every bolt she is given after a river. *Speech habit:* talks only in colour words, and apologises for it, and is right more than she expects.

## Places

**The Warp House.** The long shuttered shed where the looms stand, eleven of them, in a dry dark with the warp threads under tension like a held breath. Cool, silent, and the only room in Sparable where a person is paid to look at nothing. Visitors are admitted on Thursdays and are given three minutes.

**The Assay Room.** Ground floor of the guildhall, brass and lino, where bolts are laid out and stamped and turned, and where the word *complete* is the commonest sight in the town. Cold, bright, well-lit, and the only room nobody lies in.

**The Dye Yards.** Out in the sun, north side: indigo vats, alum, woad, madder, iron, and four drying frames the size of a ship's sail. Wenna's kingdom. The smell reaches the guildhall on a south wind and the assay clerk's windows are closed all summer.

**The Great Hall.** The town's one public room, hung with the Nine Omissions, eight of them on the north wall in order, and the ninth's place taken by a length of unhemmed raw warp with a small brass label giving a date and nothing else.

**The Mending House.** Where prohibited weaves are taken. Low, grey, well-lit, and quiet, staffed by four people whose entire job is to put a thing back that cannot be put back, which they do by weaving a new thing around the hole and recording the change.

**The Spindle Yard.** Where apprentices are examined. A bare stone floor, a single loom with no warp, and an inspector who asks each child to weave one bolt with something left out of it, and then looks at the other children to see which of them can tell.

## Visual Style

A palette of undyed wool cream, madder red, woad blue, iron grey and one hot saffron, with light kept low and indirect everywhere except in the dye yards, which blaze. The Great Hall is lit by eight tall north windows and nothing else. Materials: hand-spun yarn with visible slubs, worn treadle, smooth worn beams, dyed wool drying in sun, brass stamps, a guild ribbon, waxed floor, and the specific dusty silence of a shut shed. The era reads 1870s-1900s, pre-electric: gas in the assay room only, a bicycle in the yard, a hand bell for the guild. The recurring motif is the gap — a missing stripe, a missing sleeve, a missing ninth panel, a place on a wall where something was and is now not.

## Thematic Spine

All nine stories are about the difference between what a thing is and what it has agreed to lack, and about the cost of that agreement. The accessible band treats it as perception: a child who can see, a missing reinforcement, a parade. The intermediate band treats it as record: a report, a panel, a commission. The advanced band treats it as liability: an audit, an instrument, and a clause that turns out to have been in the wall the whole time.
