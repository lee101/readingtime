# World: The Ember Covenant

Seven basalt city-states ring the caldera of Mount Sere, bound for six hundred years by a single founding promise that burned itself into the skin of everyone who swore it and kept them at peace. Daily life is a warm, loud, ash-dusted business of masons, couriers, market-traders and oath-keepers: children are taught the forms long before they are taught the sense, marriages open with a shared oath, contracts are sealed in wax over a witness-flame, and every adult wears their promises on their skin where anyone can read them. The wonderful thing is that the peace is real. The terrible thing is the fuel. The covenant burns on a single buried vein of vitrified first-fire glass, and it is six hundred years into its burning, and every city-state in the chain knows the arithmetic and none of them can agree on what to do about it.

## Setting

The caldera is nine miles across and mostly floor, and the seven cities sit on its terraces like grey teeth. Cindergate, the founder's city, is highest and smallest, cut into the vent cone itself; the terraced work-districts run down from it in a spiral, so that a person walking downhill walks through every rank of the polity without meaning to. The caldera floor is never truly dark: fissures of slow orange run under the basalt, ropes of heat shimmer rise off the streets, and snow that falls past the rim is gone before it lands. Air smells of hot stone, chimney smoke, sheep fat and turned soil. Buildings are black basalt blocks mortared with pale ash-lime, roofs of slate and copper gone green, windows of waxed horn that glow like weak tea. Everyone wears wool, felted linen and oiled leather close to the skin, because the air stays cold in the shade and too hot in the open. Food is ash-roast: root vegetables and mutton cooked in buried hot stones, brined greens, sour barley broth, smoked ewe-milk cheese. Carriage is limited to the terraced switchback roads; the fast traffic is on foot and by sledge, and the fastest of all is the courier chain, forty-six relay posts around the rim, where a sealed wax message crosses the caldera in under two hours. Market day is every fifth day at the Ninth Terrace: slate trestles, awnings of oiled sailcloth, a swap-stall where promises are traded as collateral, a bellows-smith working three coal fires at once, and, at noon, the reading of the day's Measure — the graduated stone cup filled from the covenant vent, announced aloud to everyone, because in this world the fuel level is public property and the number is repeated in the market like rain.

## The Magic

1. **Branding.** An oath sworn aloud, in the presence of a burning witness-flame, brands the skin of the swearer: a raised pale mark with an ember-coloured core, shaped like the oath's own wording — a comma for a promise to come, a bar for a promise to pay, a hooked stroke for a promise to protect. The mark is permanent and legible, and any adult can read a stranger's covenant on their forearms, throat or back in a glance.
2. **Fuel.** A brand only holds while the witness-flame is fed with first-fire glass: the vitrified slag of the founder's first eruption, found in exactly one place, a single buried vein beneath Cindergate's vent cone. No substitute is recognised by the covenant.
3. **The Measure.** Each oath's strength and duration is drawn from a finite quantity of that vein, measured in the graduated stone cup and entered in the Quiet Ledger. A great oath burns a great deal at once. An oath sworn to be kept for a hundred years costs what a hundred years of small oaths would cost, up front, on the day of swearing.
4. **Cost.** Every brand takes a toll on the body it is carved into: a small oath costs a day of life, a great oath costs a season or a decade. Most wearers show it as a flush, a grey hair, a cough, a stiffened hand. Nobody swears a large oath young; that is the whole of the covenant's economics.
5. **Limit.** An oath only brands if the swearer both intends it and is able to keep it. An oath sworn in bad faith blisters the mark and turns it black, and a black mark is not a binding: it is a public, permanent record of a broken promise, and every city-state has one.
6. **No undoing.** Oaths cannot be unmade. A brand can only be spent out by fulfilling the oath, or moved once, in the formal transfer called a Quiet Audit, to an heir who takes it up freely and knowingly. Quiet Audits are rare, strictly ceremonial, and the only legal route by which a burden changes hands.
7. **The chain.** The founder's covenant is one instrument, not nine. If any single city-state withdraws or defaults, the whole instrument loses coherence, every oath derived from it falls slack, and the fuel in the bedrock seals itself back into the ground where it will sit useless for a thousand years. Withdrawal is therefore not a political act but an extinction of everyone else's promises too.
8. **The seal.** Anything the covenant's flame touches becomes a fixture: masonry, bells, thresholds, a city gate, a person. Fixtures outlast the oath they were sworn in. A wall built by a sworn mason will still stand when the oath is spent; a road built by a sworn courier gang is still a road when the gang is gone. This is the world's one durable magic.
9. **Broken rules.** A witness-flame fed with anything other than first-fire glass does not burn blue and fail: it burns *hungry*. It draws on the nearest brands to fuel itself, blurring them a fraction, and a flame that has taken more than nine brands from one wearer leaves a mark that cannot be read and cannot be enforced. This is the reason the harvest of brands is universally taboo, and the reason the one time it was proposed, four hundred years ago, the only surviving record of it is a ledger page with the names torn out.

## Cast

**Ashara Venn**, 14, oath-keeper's apprentice at the Bonding Stair, later Keeper of the Measure. Wants to be permitted to carry the founder's own brand, which no living person has ever done. Flaw: she has convinced herself that being willing is the same as being able. Physical detail: a single fresh ember-brand on her left forearm, shaped like a comma, which she keeps covered by a linen cuff she forgets to lower. Verbal habit: she announces her own proposals in the council's own formal grammar — "Ashara proposes—" — and the doing-it-it-self makes adults wince.

**Kel Rusk**, 52, ash-mason of the Ninth Terrace, tenth generation of a family of sworn builders. Wants the terrace to still be standing in fifty years, whatever happens to the covenant. Flaw: he takes on other people's work rather than hand it back, and is so tired by midsummer he cannot climb. Physical detail: a beard burnt short on the left side and greying unevenly, a mason's bronze thumb-ring carried on a leather cord in his pocket because he will not wear it as authority. Verbal habit: answers every question with a measurement — "Fourteen courses. Three spans. Give me the barrow."

**Sister-Iven Carshal**, 60s, Keeper of the Quiet Ledger, the archive of every oath sworn since the founding. Wants the record to be true. Flaw: she edited it once, sixty years ago, and will not say what was removed. Physical detail: grey eyes gone hazy at the edges, a slate stylus kept up her left sleeve and brought out mid-sentence. Verbal habit: says "Noted," and then, two paragraphs later, quietly contradicts herself about it.

**Dain Callow**, 17, a courier of the Sixth Chain, running sealed wax between relay posts on foot. Wants to be made a keeper, and is barred from it by an old law he cannot find the reason for. Flaw: he treats laws as weather — something you outrun rather than argue with. Physical detail: bare feet with the heels scorched black, a courier's oilskin satchel with eleven worn brass buckles. Verbal habit: names every place by its relay number — "the Nine-Fork stall", "Twelve-Gate" — and corrects other people when they use names.

**Ordwin Sparre**, 34, spokesman of the Emberwastes, the grey plain outside the chain where the villages have never sworn. Wants the wastes admitted as a party to the covenant, on equal terms. Flaw: he wants to be forgiven for the toll post he burned, and cannot accept that admission and forgiveness are different things. Physical detail: a black broken-oath brand running from jaw to collarbone, silver-scarred where he has had it treated and re-treated. Verbal habit: always speaks in the plural — "we says" — even about his own childhood.

**Odo Harrow**, 41, founder and sole magistrate of Harrowfoot, the one market town in the caldera that refuses to swear. Wants his town to stay free. Flaw: he has grown rich on covenant-built roads and covenant-enforced contracts while paying none of the cost, and he knows exactly how rich. Physical detail: a burnt-cork stopper he chews when thinking, and a little book of debts he has never once collected from. Verbal habit: apologises before every demand, so that agreeing feels like being kind.

## Places

**Cindergate, the founder's terrace.** A bowl of black basalt cut into the lip of the vent cone, reached by a switchback road of nine switchbacks, with the covenant vent at its heart under a copper grille the colour of a beetle. The heat comes up through the paving in warm patches you learn to step over. At dusk the whole city smells faintly of scorched iron, because the witness-flames are lit one by one, and children race them.

**The Bonding Stair.** Nine hundred shallow basalt steps cut into the face between the first and second terrace, wide enough at the top for four abreast and at the bottom for one. The first oath was sworn on step four hundred and fifty, and the step is worn into a saddle shape. Pilgrims climb it barefoot; the climb takes most of an afternoon and people leave bread and copper wire tied to the rail, so the whole stair smells of cold bacon and candle smoke.

**The Ninth Terrace market.** Slate trestles in two long ranks under yellow-brown oiled sailcloth, a bellows-smith, a rope-stitcher, a swap-stall where an eighth-year clause is put up for trade, and at the north end a raised platform with a graduated stone cup on it. The Measure is called at noon and the number goes back into the market the way news does.

**The House of the Quiet Ledger.** A single-room archive under the terrace, walls of cooled shelves in which the city's older brands are set in black wax like specimens, six hundred years of marks in alphabetical order of oath. It is dry, cool and very quiet. The only sound in it is Sister-Iven's stylus and, sometimes, ash hissing in a wall vent, which people swear is a keeper still at work.

**The Chain Causeway.** A raised road of black basalt crossing the caldera floor from the Sixth Gate to the Third, its joints sealed with obsidian glass that has cooled to a hard bright black and catches the fissures' light like a line of dark mirrors. It is eleven miles of hard empty heat with the orange fissures running alongside, and couriers use it day and night because the nights are the only cool part.

**The Emberwastes.** The grey plain beyond the chain, three days walk from any gate, where the ground is unburned pumice and nothing grows above knee height. Settlement is a scatter of turf-roofed villages, smoke, dogs, and cairns that mark the boundary because the cairns cannot be sworn — sworn stone, the covenant says, must not be set where no one is holding it. Outsiders go in knowing they are not protected, and are careful, and are still watched.

## Visual Style

Cover art for this world is basalt, ember and heat: charcoal black and near-black blue-grey stone, ember orange and a single hot copper-red as the only saturated colour, with bone-white ash and sickly lichen-green as the neutrals. Light is always low and raking — a sun occluded by vent haze, or flames at ankle and chest height throwing long orange shadows up walls and under chins, with visible shimmer of hot air across everything. Materials are rough and honest: black basalt blocks, ash-mortar, waxed horn windows, oiled sailcloth, coarse felt and linen, brass and oxidised copper, wax seals pressed with braided-chain dies, and dusty skin. The era feel is late iron-age ritual bureaucracy — turf-roofed craftsman buildings under a vast industrial caldera, paperwork kept by people who also lay stone. The recurring motif is the comma-shaped ember-brand, glowing faintly on a forearm, a throat, or the jaw, like a coal that has not finished deciding.

## Motifs

1. The comma-ember brand — a raised pale mark with an ember-coloured core, which is a letter of the promise.
2. The Measure — a graduated stone cup filled at the covenant vent, the single number everybody in the caldera knows.
3. Obsidian glass seams, where first-fire was allowed to run into stone to fix it, and which shine like a line of dark mirrors.
4. Wax seals and brass chain-dies, with the broken seal of a covenant that has fallen slack.
5. The chain link, and specifically the one missing link that is never identified.

## Thematic Spine

Every story in this world is about the moment a promise has to be renewed by people who are not the ones who made it, and about the discovery that the thing you have all been relying on was never free — someone signed for it, and their names are still on the wall. The accessible stories take that as adventure: work, effort, and a choice made cheaply and sincerely while the adults look on. The intermediate stories take it as politics and craft — the ledger, the relay, the record, the boring machinery that keeps a covenant honest. The advanced stories take it as the hardest question there is: whether a settlement is made honest by a working miracle or by the dull public work of many small people, and who pays while the answer is being decided.
