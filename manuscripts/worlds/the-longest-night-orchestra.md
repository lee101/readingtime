# World: The Longest Night Orchestra

High in the Vellmark range, above the last tree line and below the first snow, a ring of mortared stone stands on a windswept shoulder called the Shoulder of Nine Notes. Nine great instruments are built into the ring: tuned brass horns set in the rock, silver-strung chimes the size of church bells, a bass organ whose pipes reach down into the mountain, and drums cut from the hollow heartwood of a tree that grew before there was weather. Their sound is not music for the delight of people. It is the aurora itself, and the aurora is a wall, and the wall is the Longest Night. For six hundred years the players of the Shoulder have stood their watch and played the dark back with a low ceaseless chord, and the winter has not ended once. Now there are three players where there were nine, the youngest is sixty-one years old, and the Wall has begun to lean. The untried solution sits in the oldest part of the ring: a note that has never been played, which the Founding Hours call the Ending Note and which every player has been forbidden from learning. The question the nine stories keep circling is the same one the mountain is asking — whether ending the winter forever is a mercy, and what it costs the ones who end it.

## Setting

The Vellmark range is a high cold shoulder of black rock and pale scree, held above a valley of dark pine three thousand feet below. The Shoulder of Nine Notes is a ring wall of mortared granite, nine metres high, built in nine segments with a gap in each, so the sound moves rather than bounces. The wind here is a steady west wind with a whistle in it. There is no snow that melts; frost forms in a fine white ash that stings the eyes. Below the Shoulder, Sillvane is the only settlement: two hundred people, turf-roofed houses, a peat fire in every house burning all year, a bell tower whose bell has been silent for six hundred years because no one has been able to tune it properly without an aurora-player to listen to. Bread is dense grey rye. The diet is reindeer, cloudberry, smoked fish from the lake three valleys over, and a sour milk soup that the whole valley calls weather. Everyone in Sillvane knows the words of the Standing Chord. Everyone sings it indoors, quietly, never at night. Market day is every ninth day at the Windward Yard, where the aurora-players come down from the Shoulder to trade, and which is the only time in the year that Sillvane is loud.

## The Magic

1. **The instruments are bound to the mountain, not to their players.** Anyone may be called to the ring. No one may leave it. The horn stops answering a hand that tries to walk off the shoulder.
2. **The aurora is a chord, not a light.** It is held in the sky the way a held note is held in a throat. The visible green and cold-blue curtain is only the sound made visible, and it thins where the chord thins.
3. **The Standing Chord may not stop.** From dusk to dawn in the Longest Night months, the ring must sound. A gap in the chord is a gap in the wall. The wall does not attack; it simply is not there, and what is behind it is not winter but unending.
4. **Music costs the player.** Every hour a player holds a note, that hour is taken from the player's own warmth. Players in the deep months age visibly and are cold to the touch. A player who plays too long past their span grows still, and still players are not mourned, they are simply removed from the roster.
5. **No one may stop to rest within a chord.** Rest is a rest between notes. The Standing Chord has no rests in it. This is why the players are thin, grey, and hoarse.
6. **The circle must be nine.** The wall is tuned in nine parts and it is held in nine. The ring with fewer than nine players is already a hole, and the hole widens.
7. **The Ending Note may be learned only by a player who has freely given up a year of their own life to the ring.** A life given to the ring cannot be taken back, and cannot be given by proxy, and cannot be paid for by the mountain.
8. **The Ending Note ends the winter once and for all, and it does not come back.** The night lifts and does not return. What the Year was before the First Chord — the ordinary, un-walled, un-auditioned sky — becomes the sky again. Nobody in the story gets to have both.
9. **When the rules are broken, the chord keeps the shape of the break.** Play the same note twice where the ring was built to hold a silence, and that silence becomes a permanent room in the mountain. Play out of turn, and the piece moves on without you and your place in it is taken by the wind. A player who lies to the ring is heard by the ring; the ring does not distinguish between a lie and a wrong note.
10. **The mountain remembers every chord ever played in it.** The Founding Hours are not a book. They are the sound of the whole previous six hundred years, and they can be heard in a sustained note by a player who holds long enough to get cold enough to listen.

## Cast

1. **Maran Ostler** — 61, the last of the young-ones, a horn-line player appointed at nineteen and never once relieved. Wants to be allowed to stop playing. Flaw: has never once been relieved, and has quietly organised everything so that no one else ever has to be. Detail: her left hand is permanently white to the second knuckle, and she wears a mitten with the thumb cut off so she can work the valves. Speech habit: ends every sentence with the name of the note she is about to play — "I'll take the third. The third is fine."
2. **Sef Anhalt** — 34, a chimerunner and striker, came up to the ring at twenty-three for a six-month labour term and has been there eleven years. Wants to leave and go back down to Sillvane. Flaw: he is the best tuner the ring has ever had and he knows it, and he cannot stop tuning. Detail: a burn scar in the shape of a tuning fork across the web of his right hand. Speech habit: calls everyone by their instrument, not their name — "Horn-line. Bass. Small-bell."
3. **Ysolde Vant** — 72, the last of the silver-chime keepers, the oldest living player, walked to the shoulder at fifty-five with frost in her lungs. Wants to die on the ring while it is still standing. Flaw: she is the ring's memory and the ring has been teaching her the Ending Note whether she asked or not. Detail: her breath is audible from across the ring, a faint dry whistle in and out. Speech habit: speaks in a continuous ceremonial cadence, never interrupting herself, as though reciting something written down.
4. **Pell Underhay** — 15, an apprentice, the ninth player, appointed two years ago after her mother played and was stilled. Wants to be told she is the ring's equal and not its charity. Flaw: she wants it so badly that she has begun to count the cost of other people's lives in a ledger. Detail: a ledger notebook with a broken spine carried inside her coat, filled in pencil, columns of dates. Speech habit: asks "what is the number for that" when she does not know how to say something is wrong.
5. **Kell Ostler** — 58, Maran's older brother, formerly a bass-pipe player, removed from the ring in his twenties and living in Sillvane, where he mends harnesses. Wants Maran to come home before he is completely gone. Flaw: he is ashamed of the relief he feels that it was him and not her, and has spent thirty years trying to be forgiven for it. Detail: a badly mended left glove, the thumb-lining always showing. Speech habit: apologises for taking up the air, and finishes other people's sentences.
6. **Wend Cassock** — 43, the last of the collectors, who walks the ring at every change of watch and records what each note has done to the mountain. Wants the ledger to be a true record and nothing more. Flaw: has begun writing down what she believes the Ending Note will do, as fact, without having played it. Detail: a brass survey lantern and a folding rule on a cord, worn on her hip. Speech habit: narrates other people's actions aloud in the third person, quietly, to write them down.

## Places

- **The Shoulder of Nine Notes** — A ring wall of grey mortared granite, nine metres high, broken into nine segments with narrow gaps, standing on a bare slab of black rock in a wind that never drops. From the inside it looks like standing in a roofless round room with the sky as the missing floor.
- **The Bass Mouth** — The great organ's central pipe, a black iron throat a person can stand upright inside, descending into a shaft where the rock is warm and the walls shine with a thin film of green. It is the coldest-sounding place in the world and the safest.
- **Windward Yard** — The broad gravel shelf in the valley below where the market days happen, ringed with hitching posts and stone fire-pits. On a market day it is the loudest place under the mountain, and the only place where talking about the ring is normal.
- **The Gallows Stair** — The cut-stone stair of two hundred and forty steps from the valley floor up to the ring, worn into shallow bowls in the middle of each tread. From the top step you can see the whole wall of the aurora at once, and it is the reason nobody climbs it for pleasure.
- **The Lacuna Rooms** — The rooms the mountain makes where a broken rule has been played badly: smooth, cold, absolutely silent chambers behind the wall, holding a single note forever. They are furnished with the ringing of your own voice if you speak in one.
- **Sillvane** — Two hundred turf-roofed houses in a sheltered fold of the valley, peat smoke standing straight up in the still cold, and a silent bell in a tower nobody rings. It is warm, cramped, gossipy, and entirely ordinary, and every player in the ring has come from it.

## Visual Style

Cold-blue and aurora green as the only saturated colour, everything else in the value range of wet slate, black iron, frost-white ash, and old tarnished brass. Lighting is always low and lateral: the light comes from the sky itself, so faces are lit from above and behind in shifting green, with the stonework in near-silhouette. Materials are stone, iron, brass, bone-white antler, and heavy wool in undyed greys and browns, with lichen and frost in every crevice. The era feel is pre-industrial and ceremonial — no machines, no glass windows above the tree line, hand-worked metal, everyone in wool and hide. The recurring motif is a single unbroken line: the horn, the chime, the rail, the horizon, the edge of the aurora, drawn in all nine stories.

## Motifs

1. The single unbroken line — horn tube, chime wire, stair rail, aurora edge.
2. Frost that forms in a fine white ash rather than as ice.
3. A ledger, tally, or written column of names and dates.
4. The two hands of a tuner's claw hammer, and gloves with the thumb cut off.
5. Green light held inside a black iron pipe.

| 104 | Sef and the Two True Notes | intermediate | short | A tuning error has put the ring half a tone sharp, and the only way to correct it is to silence the chimes while the drones hold, which Sef must do by hand with a padded mallet in ninety seconds of open sky. He succeeds and the sky thins, and he has to decide what to do with the two hours of night he now believes he caused. |
| 105 | Nine Hands on the Drum | intermediate | medium | The heartwood drum has begun to rot from the inside and cannot be played without splitting, and the only sound that would fill the gap is the long held note Sef has just learned the meaning of. | 106 | The Lacuna Below the Wall | intermediate | long | Pell wants to see the rooms a broken rule makes, so she plays a silence where a note belongs and goes looking, and Ysolde follows her because Ysolde has been hearing that room for years. What is in the rooms is a version of the ring played by nobody, and it wants the ninth place, and the two of them have to break their own silence to get out. |
| 107 | A Year, Given | advanced | short | Ysolde has learned the Ending Note in the sound of the mountain and only has to find a player who will freely give a year. | 108 | The Roll of the Registry | advanced | medium | The circle is nine and there are four, and the missing five are dead, stilled, or walked off the shoulder and taken by the wind. Wend discovers the Foundation can be rewritten from the record, that the ring will accept anyone who is written in, and she begins, over a long winter, filling the roll with the names of people from Sillvane who volunteer without knowing what they are agreeing to. |
| 109 | The Note That Was Never Played | advanced | long | The only Ending Note is a note the ring was built to hold as a silence, and the mountain will not teach it to anyone who wants to end the winter — it will only teach it to someone who will let go of the wall as well as the night. The ninth story puts all six of them on the Shoulder for the last night, where the resolution is not a victory but a choice about what kind of sky is worth having, and no one in it gets both things. |

## Thematic Spine

All nine stories are about the difference between holding something and being held, and about whether a duty that lasts six hundred years still belongs to the people inside it. The accessible level treats it as loyalty and warmth — the small hands that keep the small bell moving, the one visit that is allowed down the stair. The intermediate level treats it as consequence — who pays, in cold, hours, and arithmetic, when a shared work is spread thinner and thinner. The advanced level treats it as consent — a note that can only be played by someone who gives up a year, a registry that can be filled by people who never read what they signed, and the discovery that the ending the ring is built to deliver is the ending of the wall too.
