# The Dream That Belonged to Her
![Cover art: The Dream That Belonged to Her](../art/intermediate/14-the-dream-that-belonged-to-her.webp)

Aurelia woke with a feather in her mouth.

The feather was green and soft. It rested on her tongue when she opened her eyes. A bright thread led from it to the window, where dust floated in sunlight.

She sat up.

The bed was made of moon-white wood. A crown waited on the table beside it. Beyond the curtains, a kingdom stood silent beneath a blue sky.

Aurelia did not remember her name.

She remembered a hallway. She remembered red shoes. She remembered someone saying, “The kingdom will remember for you.”

She removed the feather. The thread snapped.

In that moment, every clock in the palace began to ring.

* * *

Queen Elara met Aurelia in the Hall of Stories. The queen wore years stitched in green thread.

“You have woken,” she said.

“I think I have.”

“You slept one hundred years.”

“Who am I?”

The queen lifted one hand. A painted scene appeared on the wall. It showed a girl with auburn hair touching a spindle in a tower. The girl’s face was Aurelia’s face.

“You are the Sleeping Princess,” the queen said. “You slept so the kingdom could sleep. The villagers are safe. The fields are green. The river has not flooded.”

The scene changed to a market. A woman sold apples. A child chased a dog. Behind them, a house stood with a blue door.

Aurelia leaned closer. “That house looks familiar.”

“It is your childhood home. Your family still lives there.”

“Is it?”

The queen’s fingers moved. The picture softened. “Your family has many stories. Some are painful. The kingdom has chosen which ones may be shared.”

Aurelia touched the wall.

“My memories?”

“Safe in the Archive. The story of your sleep is our spine. We removed the unclear parts.”

“Unclear?”

The queen opened a cabinet. Glass jars held a ribbon, the smell of rain, and a pair of small hands.

Aurelia reached for the jar labeled HER FIRST FRIEND.

The lid was sealed with wax.

“Can I see it?”

“Of course.” The queen handed it to her.

Aurelia shook the glass. Nothing moved. She held it to the light. A small red kite appeared inside, folded like a sleeping bird.

“I cannot remember a kite.”

“That is why it is preserved.”

“Preserved for whom?”

The queen met her eyes. “For the story.”

Aurelia set down the jar. “I do not want a story made from things I cannot touch.”

* * *

The Archive stood beneath the palace.

Aurelia walked down stone stairs with the queen. The walls held shelves from floor to ceiling. On each shelf rested a labeled object.

A cough. A footprint. A loaf of bread with one bite missing. A blue mitten. A message rewritten four times.

Aurelia stopped at a shelf marked LOST EVENINGS.

She remembered serving soup to a cat. She remembered rain on the palace roof. She remembered Pella, who had promised to meet her by the fountain.

“Who is Pella?” the queen asked.

Aurelia had not spoken the name aloud before. “My friend.”

The queen frowned. “That is not in the official account.”

Aurelia picked up the blue mitten. A memory entered her hand. She saw Pella beneath a willow and heard herself say, “Keep the pit. We will meet in the same dream next week.”

Pella smiled. “I will bring the seed.”

Aurelia dropped the mitten. The memory sank away, leaving the taste of peach on her tongue.

“Where is Pella now?”

The queen looked through a book. “There is a note that she became a gardener.”

“Did she find me?”

The pages moved. “Your visit was removed.”

“From the memory?”

“From the public story.”

Aurelia felt anger warm her face. “A memory is not public.”

“Everything in a kingdom becomes a story sooner or later.”

Aurelia took the blue mitten. “Then some stories should stay with the people who lived them.”

A sound shook the shelves. Jars trembled. Their labels blurred.

The queen caught Aurelia’s arm. “If you free every memory, the past will fall in without order.”

“I am not asking to destroy it.”

“You are making room for what was difficult.”

“Yes.”

Aurelia passed KING’S FIRST PROMISE and KING’S FIRST BROKEN PROMISE.

“What happened to the broken one?”

“Your father removed it. He said a kingdom needs a clean beginning.”

Aurelia withdrew her hand. “I do not want what belongs to someone else.”

The queen looked surprised. “What do you want?”

Aurelia thought of the feather, the green thread, and the queen’s painted scenes.

“The parts that are mine. Only the parts that are mine.”

* * *

The Archive opened its deepest door. A room filled with paper birds waited beyond it.

A small keeper sat among them.

“You are the royal editor,” Aurelia said.

“I edit what the kingdom remembers.”

“From the night I slept?”

“From the night before.”

She touched a bird labeled THE GIRL WHO WAS ASLEEP.

A young girl held a candle. A spindle dropped. An old woman caught the fire. A prince cut through ivy, but images had gaps. No footsteps. No nurse. No door.

“The prince was not there when I touched the spindle,” Aurelia said.

“No. He arrived later. He was chosen for the ending.”

Aurelia’s hands tightened. “I was not a princess because he found me.”

“The kingdom needed a clear story.”

“I needed the truth.”

Aurelia took the bird.

“If I open it, it will not return in order,” the keeper warned.

“I will find my order.”

Light filled the room. Aurelia felt cold stone, the spindle rolling away, and her hand burning. The old woman caught her before the flame reached the curtain.

Then came the waiting. She read books, watched clouds, and spoke with Pella through a crack. Pella promised a seed but never came.

One day a young man stood below the tower. The official story called him a prince, but Aurelia did not know his name. He cut the ivy, climbed, saw her asleep, and left.

“Why?” she asked.

“The story needed him to kiss you,” the keeper said.

“Did he choose that?”

“No.”

The queen followed. “The boy is dead now. His kingdom still tells the kiss.”

Aurelia held the small card inside the bird. “Keep his memory for the people who knew him. The moment belongs to me.”

Her own name was the first to return: Aurelia.

* * *

The kingdom gathered in the square.

Aurelia stood on the palace steps. The queen sat beside her, and the keeper held a book without a cover.

The people had been told that the princess would choose the kingdom’s official memory. Flags and ribbons filled the square.

Aurelia opened the book.

“This is the first memory,” she said. “I was a child. I was lonely. I touched a spindle that I did not understand. I was hurt. I was afraid.”

A murmur moved through the square.

“My family left bread outside my door. I smelled it through the crack. I never saw who brought it.”

The queen looked down. The story had been removed because it was too small for a kingdom.

“I spoke with Pella. She promised a seed. She did not come. I do not know why, and I will not invent a reason.”

A woman in the crowd began to cry. Aurelia did not look away.

“And this is the fourth. A boy climbed the tower. He did not kiss me. He left when he saw I was asleep. The kiss belongs to the story people made later. It does not belong to me.”

“How will we remember the prince?” a man called.

Aurelia lifted the small card. “We can remember the boy without stealing the rest. A story can hold more than one truth if it has room.”

The square was quiet. Some people looked angry. Some looked relieved.

Aurelia continued, “I will not return every memory. Some belong to my family, my friends, or people who loved someone I never met. I will not force them to surrender them.”

“Open only the drawers marked MINE,” she told the keeper.

The queen nodded. Aurelia’s jars glowed; small lights rose through the windows, carrying lost buttons, red scarves, and a dog barking at the moon.

The official book shook. A painted page tried to close itself.

“Someone has written a new ending,” the keeper said.

Aurelia read: *The princess returned the kingdom’s perfect story.*

“No. I am returning the parts I choose.”

She crossed out *perfect*. The ink spilled.

*Perfect* became *shared.*

The pictures loosened, making room for the people who had lived inside them.

The queen rose. “Will you rule?”

“I will listen first,” Aurelia said. “Then I will decide.”

The crowd cheered. It was not a cheer for a finished story, but for a door left open.

That night, Aurelia opened her window. The feather lay on the pillow, its new barb soft as grass.

Across the fields, other windows glowed. In some rooms, people held forgotten objects. In others, they sat with private memories.

Aurelia let the feather rise into the sky. Its green thread drew a crooked path among the stars, less like a crown than a way forward.

She did not follow it. She chose to sleep in the moonlight, carrying only the memories that had asked to come home.
